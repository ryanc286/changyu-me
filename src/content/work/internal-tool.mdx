At Carousell, the marketing team needed engineers for almost every campaign. I designed three internal tools that let them do it on their own. Each one works by itself, but together they work like plug-and-play blocks, turning technical tasks into simple, visual steps.

## How the blocks fit

A landing page is the frame. Special Collections and LeadGen forms are blocks that drop into it, next to stories and images. Marketing can build a collection, add a survey, and put both on a campaign page, all without a ticket to engineering.

## Special Collections

To promote a category, marketing had to ask engineers to set up a listing collection in the backend. Every change meant another round of back and forth. I interviewed the team to learn what they needed to see first, then designed a tool to create and manage collections themselves.

<Media id="collections" label="Creating and managing a collection" />

- **Set up in one place.** A new collection starts with its type and its start and end dates, grouped in the order marketers think about them.
- **Find it fast.** Search, plus filters by type and date, since promotions run for set periods.
- **Add in bulk.** Upload listings with a CSV file, or pick them by category and keyword.
- **Pin what matters.** Marketing asked for a way to feature key listings, so pinned listings move to the top of the collection in the app.

## Landing Page Builder

Campaign pages were often squeezed onto listing pages. They were rigid and easy to miss. I reviewed past campaigns and talked to the team about how they built them. Three patterns covered most cases: collections, stories and images. I made them stackable and reorderable, like LEGO blocks, so marketers can build a page for any campaign.

<Media id="builder" label="Building a campaign page from blocks" />

The settings follow the order marketers set up a campaign, and the pages work on both web and app. The team's feedback after release was positive. Scheduling and preview are planned next, so campaigns go live on time and mistakes get caught before publishing.

## LeadGen

As the business grew, we needed a way to run surveys and collect leads at scale, and third-party tools were costly and hard to change. I looked at other tools and asked the team which forms and question types they used most. The result is an in-house form builder where questions can be dragged into any order.

<Media id="leadgen" label="Building a survey with drag and drop" />

## What changed for marketing

Setting up a campaign takes less time, since marketers no longer wait on engineers for each step. They also have more tools to work with: collections, campaign pages and surveys they can mix for each campaign.

## What I learned

Designing for business users is still user-centered design. Interviews and workflow reviews showed that marketers needed two things: less manual work, and confidence that a campaign was set up right. A clear hierarchy, little decoration and consistent interactions made complex tasks feel simple, and feedback after each release guided the next round.
