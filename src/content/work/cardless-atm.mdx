Over half of payments in Japan are still made in cash. PayPay wanted the app to be the only thing you need, at the ATM too. I redesigned the ATM flow so people can withdraw and deposit cash with just their phone, across two screens with very different rules.

## Where people got stuck

I watched people use ATMs in person, sorted support tickets with our CS team, and went through the funnel with a data analyst. Three moments stood out:

- **Getting started.** Setup asked for several checks with unclear steps. 40% of users failed before they even reached an ATM.
- **Switching screens.** After scanning the QR code, the app showed a number to type on the ATM, but never told people to look at the ATM. 13% gave up here.
- **Not knowing if it worked.** The app could not show the amount or the new balance. That uncertainty drove 7% of support tickets.

## What I held the design to

All three problems came from the same place: the app made people do work it should have done for them. So I set three rules before designing anything.

- **As simple as a cash card.** Setup was where 40% failed, so the app should carry that work, not the user.
- **Add, don't disrupt.** People already knew the app. New options should fit in, not make them relearn it.
- **Be honest about limits.** The app can't show the amount yet. Say so, and point people to where they can check.

## Design

Most competitors used the same flow: scan, get a number, type it in. The real difference was guidance, telling people when to switch from the phone to the ATM. That became our focus.

<Media id="competitors" label="A competitor's cardless ATM flow: scan, get a number, type it in" src="/images/cardless-atm/competitors.webp" />

The first screen, where you choose withdraw or deposit, sets the tone for every step after it. I tested three versions with 8 people: icons on each button, a horizontal layout, and an illustration. Standing at an ATM, often with people waiting behind them, users need to tell the options apart without reading. Icons did that. The illustration added noise, and the vertical layout matched the rest of the app. Icons won.

<Media id="concepts" label="Three versions of the service selection screen" src="/images/cardless-atm/concepts.webp" />

<Media id="flow" label="Set up, choose, scan, enter the number, done" />

Each step answers one of the problems above. Setup shows clear steps and progress. After scanning, the app tells people to look at the ATM and type the number there, so nobody gets stuck between screens. At the end, the app can't show the amount yet, so it says so plainly and points people to where they can check it.

The ATM screen came with strict vendor rules: a few colors, fixed font sizes and fixed layout zones. With so little to work with, hierarchy did the work. Amount, balance, fees and limits are what people glance at mid-transaction, so those come first.

## What the beta showed

In beta with 10 to 20% of users, people did something we did not plan for. They started at the ATM and used the app's general scan button, so the app did not know which service they had picked. I added a check: if a user has no linked PayPay Bank account, the app sends them to e-money top-up, with no extra steps or errors. A short tutorial helps people find the new flow.

## What I learned

Ask "what if people don't follow the flow?" before launch, not after. Two devices means two contexts, each with its own mood and time pressure. And strict limits helped: every element on the ATM screen had to earn its place.

Next, I want the app to show the amount and status once the ATM confirms the transaction, so every withdrawal has a clear end, and to add haptic feedback when people enter the number.
