Over half of payments in Japan are still made in cash. PayPay wanted the app to be the only thing you need, at the ATM too. I redesigned the ATM flow so people can withdraw and deposit cash with just their phone, across the phone and the ATM, each with very different rules.

## Where people got stuck

I started at the ATM, testing the cardless flow myself. I noted where I hesitated, where instructions didn't land, and where switching between phone and ATM felt disorienting. Then I worked with our CS team to group support tickets into failure types, and went through the funnel with a data analyst. The problems weren't random. They clustered around three moments:

- **Getting started.** Setup asked for several checks with unclear steps. 40% of users failed before they even reached an ATM.
- **Switching screens.** After scanning the QR code, the app showed a number to type on the ATM, but never told people to look at the ATM. 13% gave up here.
- **Not knowing if it worked.** The app could not show the amount or the new balance. That uncertainty drove 7% of support tickets.

## What I held the design to

All three problems came from the same place: the app made people do work it should have done for them. So I set three rules before designing anything.

- **As simple as a cash card.** Setup was where 40% failed, so the app should carry that work, not the user.
- **Add, don't disrupt.** People already knew the app. New options should fit in, not make them relearn it.
- **Be honest about limits.** The app can't show the amount yet. Instead of hiding that, confirm what we can and point people to where they can check.

## Design

Before designing, I looked at how other apps handled cardless ATMs. Most used the same basic flow: scan a QR code, get a number, type it in. But almost none told people when to switch from the phone to the ATM. So we didn't need a new flow. We needed better guidance at the handoff.

<Media id="competitors" label="A competitor's cardless ATM flow: scan, get a number, type it in" src="/images/cardless-atm/competitors.webp" />

Setup came first, since 40% of users failed there. I broke it into clear steps with visible progress, so people always know where they are and what to do next.

The next screen, where you choose withdraw or deposit, sets up every step after it. I tested three versions with 8 people. At an ATM, often with people waiting behind them, users need to tell the options apart without reading. Icons on a vertical list did that best, and matched the rest of the app. The illustration only added noise.

<Media id="concepts" label="Three versions of the service selection screen" src="/images/cardless-atm/concepts.webp" />

Then the handoff, the moment we set out to fix. After scanning, the app now tells people to follow the steps on the ATM. That one line closes the gap where 13% used to give up.

<Media id="handoff" label="After scanning, the app tells people to turn to the ATM" src="/images/cardless-atm/handoff.webp" />

The last screen does what the third rule promised: it confirms the transaction went through and shows where to check the details.

## The ATM screen

The other half of the handoff is the ATM itself. Its screen came with strict rules from the vendor and from regulations: a few colors, fixed font sizes and fixed layout zones. There was no design system to lean on and no mobile patterns to borrow. So hierarchy did the work. Someone glancing at the ATM mid-transaction should see their balance, the fee and the limit, and nothing else. I also gave fraud warnings a fixed spot on the screen, since scammers were tricking people into sharing their ATM QR code.

<Media id="atm-screen" label="The ATM screen: fee, balance and limits at a glance" src="/images/cardless-atm/atm-screen.webp" />

## What the beta showed

In beta with 10 to 20% of users, people did something we didn't plan for: they started at the ATM and opened the app's general scan button, so the app didn't know which service they wanted. Instead of asking them to change the habit, I worked with engineers to route them by default: no linked PayPay Bank account means e-money top-up, with no extra steps. I also added a short tutorial so people find the new flow first.

## Results

Each number maps back to where people got stuck. A simpler setup, where 40% used to fail, brought more people into the service. Clear guidance at the handoff and a clear last screen meant more transactions finished. And with less to figure out, a visit to the ATM got faster than using a card.

## What I learned

Ask "what if people don't follow the flow?" before launch, not after. Two screens mean two contexts, each with its own mood and time pressure. And strict limits helped: every element on the ATM screen had to earn its place.

Next, I want the app to show the amount and status once the ATM confirms the transaction, so every withdrawal has a clear end, and to add haptic feedback when people enter the number.
